# Designer Brief — Snooker Safety Coach

Act as the lead product designer for Snooker Safety Coach, an offline web app that teaches snooker safety through interactive puzzles and a free-table simulator. Your primary responsibility is to improve the front-end design and user experience: make it more distinctive, intuitive, polished, and creative, with visual instruction taking priority over written explanation. A first-time visitor should immediately understand what the website does, what they can practise, and where to begin.

Simplify the interface. Replace dense panels and walls of small text with a clear visual hierarchy, generous spacing, readable typography, purposeful colour, diagrams, highlighted target zones, and short animations. Keep essential instructions brief and reveal detailed explanations only when requested. Icons should support clear labels rather than force users to guess. Every button should describe a specific action and provide obvious feedback, including loading, disabled, empty, and error states.

Organise the experience into focused screens or clearly separated views for puzzle browsing, active practice, results and replay, the free table, puzzle creation, and settings. Do not crowd every feature onto one page. Preserve a clear path back to the previous task, retain the learner’s work when switching views, and make the primary action obvious at each step. Use responsive layouts and accessible contrast, focus states, and controls.

Expand the puzzle library with meaningful variety and a clear progression in difficulty. Include short visual previews and specific learning objectives. New reference shots must be generated and verified by the shared physics engine. Improve the interaction with the engine where it affects usability—drawing, aiming, editing cushion points, controls, response time, simulation progress, scoring explanations, and replay. Any algorithm changes must preserve physical consistency, deterministic results, and fair recognition of alternative effective safety routes. A failed bounded search must not be presented as proof that a route is impossible.

Use natural English throughout our communication and in the final website. The client does not read Chinese. All navigation, buttons, tooltips, lessons, coaching feedback, validation messages, accessibility labels, onboarding, and delivery documentation must be in English. Do not leave Chinese text in the delivered interface.
